Filter cartridges

Wound Filter Cartridges

Controlled yarn-wound depth cartridges for economical particulate removal, high solids capacity and compatibility-led material selection.

Operating principle

How the filtration product works

Wound cartridges are made by continuously winding filtration yarn around a perforated core in a controlled pattern. The overlapping yarn creates tapered flow channels and a depth matrix. Feed moves from outside to inside, depositing larger particles in the outer zones while progressively finer pathways retain smaller material.

Winding tension, yarn type, core, density and pattern establish the filtration behaviour. A well-matched wound cartridge can offer robust dirt holding at economical cost in high-solids liquid duties.

Selection starts with the process.

State the liquid or gas, operating temperature, target retention, batch volume, viscosity, solids load, housing geometry and cleaning or disposal requirement.

Media design

Construction and process behaviour

Available constructions include polypropylene yarn with a polypropylene core and cotton yarn with a stainless-steel core. Raw materials are processed into controlled fibres and rovings before winding. Additional material combinations can be evaluated for specific requirements.

Core and yarn must both be chemically and thermally compatible. Cotton may suit selected oils, dilute acids, solvents and potable-liquid duties, while polypropylene is suited to many organic acids, oils, concentrated alkalis, water, solvents and electroplating solutions—always subject to compatibility review.

Process fit

Applications and selection considerations

PP wound cartridges are suited to many organic acids, oils, concentrated alkalis, water, organic solvents and electroplating solutions. Cotton wound constructions serve selected vegetable oils, dilute acids, organic solvents, potable liquids and alkalis. Final material selection is based on full process compatibility.

Why trial a wound cartridge?

  • Actual yarn swelling can change flow and retention.
  • Soft or deformable particles may penetrate differently from rigid test dust.
  • Pressure-driven channeling can occur if construction or support is unsuitable.
  • Product fibres or gels may blind outer layers prematurely.
  • Core chemistry can be as critical as the wound yarn.

Questions engineers ask

Frequently asked questions

What controls the micron rating of a wound cartridge?

Yarn properties, winding pattern, tension and density create the depth pathways. The rating is defined by the applicable performance test method.

Why choose cotton with a stainless-steel core?

That combination may suit selected process fluids or temperatures where cotton is preferred and a strong corrosion-resistant core is appropriate.

Can wound cartridges be cleaned and reused?

They are generally treated as replaceable depth media. Validated reuse is difficult because solids lodge inside the winding.

What causes fibre release?

Incompatible chemistry, excessive differential pressure, weak yarn, poor winding or incorrect flow can contribute. Use the specified direction and limits.
