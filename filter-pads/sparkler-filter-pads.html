Filter pads

Sparkler Filter Pads for Horizontal Plate Filters

Cut media for sparkler filter assemblies, selected around plate geometry, fluid compatibility, solids loading, clarification target and cycle economics.

Operating principle

How the filtration product works

A horizontal plate sparkler filter contains stacked plates with perforated support screens. Feed enters the closed shell under pump pressure and is distributed across the plate stack. The supported pad or cloth retains solids while filtrate passes through the media, enters the central interlocking-cup channel and leaves the filter.

As deposited solids form a cake, resistance rises and flow commonly falls at constant pump conditions. The useful cycle ends when throughput, differential pressure or filtrate quality reaches the agreed limit. Correct pad diameter, centre-hole geometry, sealing and flow direction are essential to prevent bypass.

Selection starts with the process.

State the liquid or gas, operating temperature, target retention, batch volume, viscosity, solids load, housing geometry and cleaning or disposal requirement.

Media design

Construction and process behaviour

Sri Bharathi publishes disposable and reusable sparkler media in woven and nonwoven constructions. Referenced materials include polypropylene, nylon, cotton and polyester, with specialty fabrics available for particular duties. Heat-sealed or reinforced edges may be used to improve durability.

Media selection should account for chemical compatibility, temperature, slurry abrasiveness, required cake release, cleanability and the risk of fibre migration. Cellulose pulp pads may also be cut for supported sparkler duties where depth clarification is required.

Process fit

Applications and selection considerations

Sparkler filter pads are used extensively in pharmaceutical and chemical processing and can be evaluated for food, beverage, oil and gelatin clarification when material and document requirements are met. They are typically chosen for batch clarification, catalyst or particulate recovery, polishing and protection of downstream filters.

Data needed for selection

  • Equipment make, plate diameter, centre-hole size and pad drawing
  • Fluid composition, pH, temperature and solvent exposure
  • Viscosity, solids concentration and approximate particle distribution
  • Target filtrate clarity, initial flow and acceptable cycle time
  • Disposable versus reusable preference and cleaning method

Questions engineers ask

Frequently asked questions

Why does sparkler-filter flow decrease during a batch?

The retained solids form an increasingly resistant cake. Flow falls unless the pump raises differential pressure; operation should remain within equipment and media limits.

Can a cloth and a cellulose pad be substituted directly?

Not automatically. They may differ in thickness, compressibility, sealing, retention, cake release and pressure-drop behaviour.

What causes bypass in a sparkler filter?

Incorrect OD or centre-hole dimensions, damaged media, poor plate alignment, inadequate sealing, folds or an incorrect assembly sequence can allow unfiltered paths.

Should sparkler pads be pre-wetted?

That depends on the media and process. If pre-wetting is used, the wetting fluid, volume and disposal or recovery procedure should be defined in the operating method.
