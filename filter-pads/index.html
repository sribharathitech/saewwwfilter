Industrial Filter Pads for Process Clarification

Sri Bharathi manufactures cellulose-based and application-specific filter pads for clarification, polishing and particulate-retention duties across industrial liquid processes.

Depth filtration explained

What industrial filter pads do

An industrial filter pad is a porous sheet or cut shape placed in a filter press, sparkler assembly or other supported filtration arrangement. Unlike a simple screen, a depth pad presents a tortuous network of fibre passages. Particles are intercepted within this matrix and, depending on the duty, at the upstream face as a filter cake develops.

Cellulose filtration media can combine clarification, solids-holding capacity and manageable flow resistance. The correct result depends on the complete grade—not on thickness or GSM alone. Fibre furnish, formation, density, porosity, impregnation, wet strength and surface condition all affect performance.

Cellulose purity and alpha cellulose

Alpha cellulose is the high-molecular-weight, alkali-insoluble portion of cellulose. Higher-purity furnish can reduce non-cellulosic extractables and improve consistency for sensitive applications, but purity alone does not define filtration efficiency. Sri Bharathi should confirm the alpha-cellulose level on the applicable grade documentation. Unverified 90% and 98% figures are intentionally not published here.

Important distinction

A pad grade is not a universal micron screen. Retention in depth media is influenced by the particle distribution, fluid properties, differential pressure and cake formation. Confirm performance by representative trials.

Selection sequence

A practical way to compare pad candidates

1

Define the feed

Record fluid chemistry, temperature, viscosity, solids concentration and particle distribution.

2

Define the result

State whether the duty is rough filtration, clarification, polishing, colour reduction or protection of downstream media.

3

Confirm the equipment

Provide filter type, plate diameter, pad drawing, flow direction, seals and available differential pressure.

4

Trial under load

Compare initial flow, pressure rise, filtrate clarity, cycle time, yield loss and pad integrity.

5

Approve the grade

Freeze the tested construction, dimensions, acceptance criteria, packing and document requirements.

6

Monitor the cycle

Trend pressure, throughput and clarity so change-out decisions are based on process performance.

Common questions

Industrial filter pad FAQs

Does a higher GSM always mean finer filtration?

No. GSM is basis weight, not a direct pore-size or micron rating. Fibre furnish, formation, density, thickness and surface treatment also influence retention and flow.

What is the difference between surface and depth filtration?

Surface filtration retains most solids at a plane or growing cake. Depth filtration captures particles through a three-dimensional fibre matrix, often providing greater solids capacity for clarification duties.

Can one pad grade handle every batch?

Not reliably. Changes in viscosity, temperature, particle distribution, pre-treatment and solids concentration can materially change pressure rise and clarity.

Why are wet and dry strength both important?

Dry strength supports converting and handling. Wet strength helps the pad retain integrity after saturation and under process pressure.

How should alpha-cellulose purity be specified?

Ask for the declared value, test method and grade-specific certificate. Treat alpha-cellulose level as one material-quality input, not a complete filtration-performance claim.
